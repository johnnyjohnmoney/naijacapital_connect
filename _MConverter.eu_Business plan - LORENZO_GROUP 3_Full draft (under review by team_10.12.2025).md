### **Business Description** {#business-description .unnumbered}

NaijaConnect Capital Company Limited is a digital financial technology
(FinTech) company that directs diaspora remittances into pre-vetted,
high-impact investment opportunities in Nigeria. The platform connects
diaspora senders with the domestic investment market. It changes the way
remittance inflows are used, turning them from consumption into
productive capital for small businesses, real estate, agriculture, and
infrastructure projects. The company's digital platform offers three
main features:

1.  Low-cost Remittance Gateway: Smooth cross-border transfers with good
    exchange rates and low transaction fees.

2.  Curated Investment Marketplace: A digital dashboard of verified
    local ventures that diaspora investors can access.

3.  Investment Monitoring and Impact Tracking: Real-time data to check
    fund performance, social impact, and return on investment (ROI).

NaijaConnect's approach focuses on transparency, thorough research, and
meeting regulations. This provides Nigerians abroad with a safe and
reliable way to invest in their home country.

## **Management & Ownership** {#management-ownership .unnumbered}

NaijaConnect will be owned jointly by the five founding partners, each
holding 15% equity at the start. Governance will follow a corporate
structure that includes:

a)  Board of Directors: Responsible for oversight, strategic
    decision-making, and policy review.

b)  Executive Management Team: Manages daily operations and carries out
    the strategic plan.

c)  Advisory Board: Made up of experts in finance, compliance,
    technology, and diaspora engagement to ensure best practices and
    follow regulations.

Key governance mechanisms and responsibilities include:

a)  The Board of Directors will oversee strategic direction and major
    decisions. A chair will be appointed from among the founders when
    the company incorporates.

b)  The Executive Management Team will handle day-to-day operations and
    implement strategy. They will report to the Board monthly through
    performance reports and key performance indicators.

c)  The Advisory Panel (External) will likely include an independent
    legal consultant, a compliance/regulatory advisor, and an industry
    advisor (fintech/asset management) to provide unbiased advice on
    licensing, risk, and product development.

d)  The CFO will manage financial controls and an internal control
    framework. A Compliance & Risk Manager will implement SEC and CBN
    compliance procedures along with AML and CTF policies.

e)  The Remuneration & Equity Policy will govern salaries, performance
    bonuses, and any future equity grants. This policy must be approved
    by the Board.

For initial capital and contributions, the founders will contribute seed
capital or in-kind support to cover the company's start-up budget. The
proposed funding requirement (outlined in the Financials section) will
determine the exact issued share capital and founders' cash
contributions before registration.

### **Market Overview** {#market-overview .unnumbered}

Nigeria's fintech and diaspora investment landscape is growing quickly.
This growth is due to strong remittance inflows, more people using
smartphones, and improved financial literacy. According to the World
Bank (2024), Nigeria received USD 20.98 billion in remittances, which
makes up nearly 6% of its GDP. The country is the largest recipient of
remittances in Africa and ranks among the top ten globally.

At the same time, fintech innovation in cross-border transfers and
digital investment has been increasing by over 22% each year (Statista,
2024). This rise is supported by better digital infrastructure,
encouragement from the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN), and a young,
tech-savvy population looking for better investment options.

NaijaConnect Capital is positioned at the center of these trends,
transforming diaspora remittances from consumption-based transfers into
productive domestic investments.

In 2025, NaijaConnect conducted a survey on diaspora investment among

findings include:

    Kingdom, United States, Canada, and the UAE, which are the top

## **BREAK-EVEN ANALYSIS** {#break-even-analysis .unnumbered}

Timeframe: Yearly (Base Case)

### **Assumptions Used** {#assumptions-used .unnumbered}

- Year 1 target users: 500 registered diaspora investors.

- Year 1 Assets Under Management (AUM) at the end of the year:
  ₦850,000,000.

- Revenue streams (Year 1, base case):

  - Management fee (2% of AUM throughout the year) = ₦34,000,000

  - Investment & advisory services = ₦7,000,000

  - Transaction & commission income = ₦5,000,000

  - Corporate Listing fees = ₦4,000,000

  - Total revenue (Year 1) = ₦50,000,000

- Cost of services / variable costs (Year 1): ₦16,500,000 (payment
  gateway fees, hosting, data costs).

- Total fixed costs (Year 1): ₦33,560,000 (Incorporation cost, wages,
  rent, insurance, etc.).

Average revenue per user (ARPU)

= ₦50,000,000 / 500 users = ₦100,000 per user / year

Average variable cost per user

= ₦16,500,000 / 500 users = ₦33,000 per user / year

### **Calculations (Base Case)** {#calculations-base-case .unnumbered}

- Contribution per user

> (Average revenue per user -- Average variable cost per user)  
>   
> = ₦ 100,000 − ₦ 33,000 = ₦ 67,000

- Contribution margin ratio  
    
  = (Total revenue − Variable costs) / Total revenue  
    
  = (₦50,000,000 − ₦16,500,000) / ₦50,000,000  
    
  = ₦33,500,000 / ₦50,000,000 = 67%

- Fixed costs (annual) = ₦33,560,000

- Total sales revenue required to break even  
    
  = Fixed costs / Contribution margin ratio  
    
  = ₦ 33,560,000 / 0.67 ≈ ₦ 50,089,552

- Number of users needed to break even  
    
  = Break-even revenue / ARPU  
    
  = ₦ 50,089,552 / ₦ 50,000 ≈ 1,005 users

# Response summary {#response-summary .unnumbered}

**Trust and Fraud/Scams:** The most significant fear is the lack of
trust and transparency, often manifesting as concerns about fraud,
scams, money being stolen or diverted, and dishonesty.

- **Financial and Economic Concerns:** Respondents are greatly concerned
  about losing their capital, negative returns on investment, currency
  fluctuations (Naira devaluation), and the value of their investment.

- **Monitoring and Accountability:** A major fear is the inability to
  monitor investments, coupled with a lack of accountability and
  mismanagement of funds.

- **Political and Security Instability:** Fears include unstable
  government policies, insecurity, poor leadership, and the uncertainty
  and instability in the political and economic environment.

- **Access to Funds and Redress:** Concerns exist around bottlenecks
  when trying to withdraw or access funds, and the lack of quick and
  guaranteed means of seeking legal redress if investments are lost.
